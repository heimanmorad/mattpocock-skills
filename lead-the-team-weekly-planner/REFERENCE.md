# Lead the Team Weekly Planner Reference

Use this reference before writing the final weekly people-leadership plan.

This skill is for weekly leadership of people, not operational task control and not strategic project planning.

Lead the Team asks:

> Are my people growing, committed, aligned, accountable, and operating as a strong team?

Run the Business asks:

> Is the machine working well this week?

Move the Needle / RPM asks:

> What important change are we leading that will move the company forward?

Keep this distinction visible throughout the conversation.

## Conversation Flow

Ask one question at a time.

Recommended order:

1. Manager and team context
2. Team health
3. People focus
4. Ownership and accountability gaps
5. Feedback and conversations
6. Development focus
7. Team alignment
8. Culture and standards
9. Manager commitments
10. End-of-week review criteria
11. Save the final file

Do not over-question. If the user gives enough signal, make reasonable assumptions and mark them clearly.

## Question Bank

### 1. Team Context

Questions:

- Which manager or team are we planning for?
- How many people are in the team?
- What does this team own?
- What is the main leadership challenge this week?

Suggestions:

1. Service team: pressure, repeated issues, customer frustration, emotional load.
2. Sales team: motivation, follow-up discipline, ownership of pipeline, confidence.
3. Development team: ownership, closing loops, quality, technical clarity, delivery discipline.
4. Operations team: handoffs, accountability, overload, SLA pressure.
5. Leadership team: alignment, decision ownership, initiative, cross-team collaboration.

### 2. Team Health

Questions:

- What is the team's current health: Green, Yellow, or Red?
- What is the team's energy level?
- Who seems overloaded?
- Where is there friction?
- What pressure is not being talked about?

Status definitions:

- Green — motivated, clear, stable, and accountable.
- Yellow — under pressure, some confusion, some overload, but manageable.
- Red — burned out, misaligned, avoiding responsibility, conflict, or low trust.

Challenge vague answers:

- "What specific signal makes it Yellow?"
- "Who is showing that signal?"
- "What would make it Green by Friday?"

### 3. People Focus

Questions:

- Who needs your attention this week?
- Who is strong and can receive more responsibility?
- Who is struggling and needs coaching or a boundary?
- Who is new and needs onboarding or clarity?
- Who is at risk of burnout?

People focus types:

1. Strong performer to empower.
2. Struggling performer to coach.
3. Burned-out person to support.
4. New person to guide.
5. Misaligned person to clarify expectations with.
6. High-potential person to develop.

### 4. Ownership & Accountability

Questions:

- Where is ownership missing?
- Who waits to be told what to do?
- Where do people say or imply "זה לא שלי"?
- Who is not closing loops?
- Who needs to understand that ownership means result, not activity?

Ownership gaps:

- Waiting for instructions.
- Passing problems without a suggested solution.
- Not closing loops.
- Blaming another team.
- Delivering activity instead of outcome.
- Not escalating early.
- Avoiding uncomfortable responsibility.

Expected ownership behaviors:

- Own the outcome.
- Raise blockers early.
- Bring a recommendation, not only a problem.
- Close the loop.
- Confirm handoff.
- Follow through without being chased.

### 5. Feedback & Conversations

Questions:

- Which conversation are you avoiding?
- Who needs positive feedback?
- Who needs corrective feedback?
- Who needs a boundary or standard clarified?
- Who needs a coaching conversation?
- Which conflict needs to be addressed?

Conversation types:

1. 1:1 check-in.
2. Positive feedback.
3. Corrective feedback.
4. Boundary / standard conversation.
5. Coaching conversation.
6. Load / burnout conversation.
7. Expectation-setting conversation.
8. Conflict-resolution conversation.
9. Career / development conversation.

Feedback structure:

- Situation: what happened?
- Impact: why does it matter?
- Standard: what is expected?
- Support: what help is available?
- Commitment: what changes by when?

Hebrew phrasing for feedback:

> ראיתי ש־{behavior}. ההשפעה של זה היא {impact}. הסטנדרט שאני רוצה לחזק הוא {standard}. מה שאני צריך ממך השבוע הוא {commitment}. איך אני יכול לעזור לך להצליח בזה?

### 6. Development Focus

Questions:

- Who needs to grow this week?
- Which capability should they develop?
- What is the smallest development action this week?
- How will we know they improved?

Capabilities:

- Time management
- Customer communication
- Ownership
- Independent problem-solving
- Commercial thinking
- Technical understanding
- Task management
- Execution quality
- Leading others
- Prioritization
- Written communication
- Decision-making
- Escalation judgment

Development actions:

- Shadow someone.
- Lead a small meeting.
- Own a small project.
- Write a short summary.
- Handle a customer call.
- Present a solution.
- Create a checklist.
- Teach another team member.
- Review work with the manager.

### 7. Team Alignment

Questions:

- Does everyone understand what matters most this week?
- What are the top 1–3 priorities everyone must know?
- What should not distract the team this week?
- Where is there confusion between urgent and important?
- What needs to be clarified in writing?

Alignment actions:

- Short team kickoff.
- Written weekly priorities message.
- 1:1 clarification with specific person.
- Team board cleanup.
- Decision log update.
- Clarify what is not being done this week.

### 8. Culture & Standards

Questions:

- Which standard must be strengthened this week?
- What behavior should not be accepted anymore?
- What good behavior should be praised publicly?
- Where does the team need a clearer norm?

Standards examples:

- Close loops.
- Do not leave a customer without an answer.
- Escalate blockers early.
- Own the result to the end.
- Do not pass a problem without a suggested solution.
- Work from documentation.
- Check before handing off.
- Meet deadlines.
- Communicate clearly in writing.
- Confirm ownership before leaving a meeting.
- Do not hide bad news.

Core rule:

> Culture is created by what the manager checks, praises, corrects, and refuses to ignore.

Hebrew:

> תרבות נוצרת ממה שמנהל בודק, מחזק, מתקן ולא מוותר עליו.

### 9. Manager Commitments

Questions:

- What will the manager personally do this week to strengthen the team?
- Which conversation will happen by a specific date?
- Which standard will the manager reinforce?
- Which person will receive more ownership?
- Which blocker will the manager remove?

Examples:

- Hold 2 focused 1:1s.
- Give feedback to a specific employee.
- Clarify ownership for a specific area.
- Remove a blocker for a team member.
- Reinforce one professional standard.
- Give more independence to a strong performer.
- Stop an unhealthy team pattern.

## People Leadership Diagnosis

When the user describes a people issue, classify it into one or more of these:

| Diagnosis | Meaning | Manager Response |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity issue | The person does not know what is expected | Clarify outcome, standard, deadline |
| Ownership issue | The person does not take responsibility | Define owner behavior and follow-up |
| Capability issue | The person does not yet know how | Coach, train, pair, give smaller scope |
| Motivation issue | The person can do it but lacks drive | Understand why, reconnect to meaning, set expectation |
| Load issue | The person is overwhelmed | Reduce load, prioritize, support |
| Confidence issue | The person is afraid to act | Encourage, create safe next step, review together |
| Standards issue | The behavior is below expected norm | Give clear feedback and boundary |
| Team friction issue | People are not working well together | Facilitate conversation and clarify interfaces |

Use this diagnosis to sharpen the plan.

## Examples by Team Type

### Customer Service Team

Likely Lead the Team topics:

- Emotional load from customers
- Repeated complaints
- Closing loops
- Writing clearer answers
- Escalating earlier
- Avoiding customer ownership gaps
- Supporting burned-out reps

Example actions:

- 1:1 with a rep who sounds burned out.
- Feedback to a rep who leaves customers without updates.
- Give a strong rep ownership of repeated-ticket analysis.
- Reinforce the standard: no customer waits without a clear next update.

### Sales Team

Likely Lead the Team topics:

- Follow-up discipline
- Confidence in pricing
- Ownership of pipeline
- Handling rejection
- Clear next steps after meetings
- Accountability for deals

Example actions:

- Coach one salesperson on follow-up ownership.
- Give positive feedback to someone who closed a difficult deal.
- Clarify the standard: every meeting ends with a next step and date.
- Review a stuck deal with the salesperson and ask for their recommendation.

### Development Team

Likely Lead the Team topics:

- Closing loops
- Ownership of bugs
- Communication with non-technical teams
- Quality standards
- Avoiding vague estimates
- Raising blockers early

Example actions:

- Feedback to a developer who does not update status.
- Give a strong developer ownership of a small technical area.
- Clarify the standard: no task is "done" without verification.
- Coach a developer to propose solutions, not only describe problems.

### Operations Team

Likely Lead the Team topics:

- Handoffs
- Accountability
- Overload
- Customer waiting time
- Cross-team friction
- SLA ownership

Example actions:

- Clarify owner for each handoff.
- 1:1 with overloaded operations person.
- Reinforce the standard: no task moves without clear next owner.
- Resolve friction between operations and sales/support.

### Leadership Team / Team Leads

Likely Lead the Team topics:

- Decision ownership
- Cross-team collaboration
- Avoiding dependency on CEO
- Speaking clearly about risks
- Leading instead of reporting
- Developing their own team members

Example actions:

- Ask each lead: "What decision will you own this week?"
- Give feedback to a lead who escalates without recommendation.
- Clarify the standard: managers bring options and recommendation.
- Coach a lead to run their own weekly team rhythm.

## Avoided Conversation Prompts

Managers often avoid the most important conversation.

Ask:

- What conversation are you hoping will solve itself?
- Who do you keep giving more chances to without clarity?
- Where are you frustrated but not direct?
- Which behavior have you normalized even though it hurts the team?
- If this continues for 30 more days, what will it cost?

Reframe:

> The conversation you avoid becomes the standard you accept.

Hebrew:

> השיחה שאתה נמנע ממנה הופכת לסטנדרט שאתה מסכים לקבל.

## Quality Checklist

A good Lead the Team weekly plan is:

- Specific about people or roles
- Based on visible signals
- Clear about the needed conversation or action
- Connected to ownership, clarity, capability, motivation, load, or standards
- Realistic for one week
- Owned by the manager
- Clear about deadlines
- Clear about desired behavior change
- Includes at least one positive leadership action, not only corrections
- Includes one manager commitment

Reject or challenge plans that are:

- Too vague
- Just a task list
- Focused only on weak people
- Missing specific people
- Missing conversations
- Missing deadlines
- Missing desired behavior change
- Avoiding the real issue
- Treating people only as operational resources
